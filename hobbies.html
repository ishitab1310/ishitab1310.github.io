Movement & rhythm

Kathak & Dance

I trained in Kathak — a classical Indian dance form that's equal parts footwork, storytelling, and devotion. There's something about the precision of it: every gesture has meaning, every beat is intentional. It's the most embodied form of language I know.

I also trained in gymnastics, which taught me a completely different relationship with my body — one built on trust and controlled recklessness. I still love to dance, any style, anywhere.

What dance taught me
That being present in your body is a skill, not a default
That performance and sincerity aren't opposites
That rhythm is a language with no translation needed
02
Visual storytelling

Photography & Video Editing

I photograph and I edit. The two go together — one is about seeing the moment, the other is about deciding what the moment means in retrospect. I find the editing process almost meditative: choosing cuts, pacing, the quiet authority of a well-timed transition.

Old Hyderabad is endlessly photogenic. I've spent a lot of time in Charminar's corridors, the evening chai stalls, the walls that look like they've survived fifty monsoons and are proud of it.

Favourite subjects
Old Hyderabad streets — texture, light, time
Solo travel frames — moments when nobody knows you
People doing ordinary things extraordinarily well
03
Words & worlds

Reading Fiction

Mostly fiction. I need books that take me somewhere else completely — stories that make the real world feel a little more possible, or a little more strange, depending on what I need that week.

Fiction is where I find the questions my research can't ask yet. It's where language does the things that linguists spend careers trying to explain, except it just does them without being asked.

What I look for in a book
Characters who feel more real than some people I know
A world I can't leave for the first hundred pages
An ending that changes how you read the beginning

Movement & exploration

Solo Travel, Tennis & More

Solo travel is its own kind of education. You're entirely responsible for what you notice, what you miss, and what you do with the unexpected. I find something deeply clarifying about being in a place where nobody knows me — no context, no history, just what I make of the next few hours.

On campus: tennis is my main sport. There's something about the back-and-forth of it — almost conversational. I'll also pick up a badminton racket when someone asks. And I paint — usually when words aren't working and I need a different kind of thinking.

The rest of the list
Painting — usually abstract, always unplanned
Puzzles — the harder the better, no shame
Singing to myself — exclusively, never for an audience
